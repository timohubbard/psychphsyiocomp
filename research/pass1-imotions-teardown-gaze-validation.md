# Pass 1: iMotions Teardown & Webcam Eye-Tracking Validation

**Research question:**

Produce a deep, cited research report to guide rebuilding an iMotions-equivalent webcam-based human-behavior research platform from the ground up. Deliverable must be BOTH (a) a functional/methodological spec reverse-engineered from iMotions and comparable platforms, and (b) a concrete engineering build guide.

Modalities in scope (all webcam/microphone based, no dedicated hardware required):
1. Screen-based eye tracking via webcam (calibration, gaze estimation, AOIs, heatmaps, fixations/saccades)
2. Respiration analysis from webcam (remote photoplethysmography / rPPG, chest-motion or facial-signal derived respiration rate, HR/HRV where relevant)
3. Voice analysis (prosody, vocal emotion/arousal, speech features)
4. Facial expression analysis (FACS action units, emotion classification, valence/arousal, engagement)

Deployment target: BROWSER-FIRST remote data collection (participants at home, own hardware, uncontrolled lighting, WebRTC/WASM/MediaPipe constraints), with a secondary in-lab desktop mode for higher-fidelity sessions and external sensor sync. Cover what breaks in the remote case and how platforms mitigate it.

Depth priorities (go deepest here):
A. VALIDATION EVIDENCE — peer-reviewed accuracy of webcam eye tracking (spatial error in degrees of visual angle, sampling rate limits), rPPG-derived respiration and heart rate vs. gold-standard (chest belt, ECG, spirometry), facial AU detection vs. certified FACS coders, and speech-emotion recognition benchmarks. Include known failure modes: lighting, head motion, glasses, skin-tone and demographic bias, frame-rate jitter, compression artifacts.
B. ANALYSIS & METRICS — the actual computed outputs: gaze metrics (time to first fixation, dwell time, revisits, fixation counts, AOI definitions, scanpaths, heatmap generation), AU-to-emotion mapping, valence/arousal, respiration rate and HRV extraction pipelines, prosodic feature sets (eGeMAPS/openSMILE), stimulus-event epoching and time synchronization across asynchronous streams, aggregation across participants, exports and statistical analysis.
C. STUDY DESIGN & RUNNING PARTICIPANTS — study/protocol design, stimulus presentation, calibration and re-calibration procedures, consent and ethics/IRB, GDPR / biometric-data law (incl. BIPA and EU AI Act implications for emotion recognition), sample size and power, counterbalancing and randomization, data-quality screening and exclusion criteria, dropout handling, participant instructions and environment setup.

Also cover: iMotions' own documented architecture, module lineup, licensing and pricing model, R&D/API and data export formats; and how it compares to alternatives (Affectiva/Smart Eye, RealEye, Tobii, GazeRecorder, WebGazer.js, OpenFace/Py-Feat, MediaPipe Face Landmarker, openSMILE, pyVHR/rPPG-Toolbox) so the build can reuse open-source components where credible.

Go deep into imotions.com (product pages, module docs, pocket guides, publications/blog, support/help center) and academic literature. Prefer primary sources and peer-reviewed validation studies over vendor marketing claims, and explicitly flag where a vendor claim is unverified.

## Summary

The verified evidence establishes a concrete, buildable feature-parity target for an iMotions-equivalent platform and a realistic accuracy budget for its gaze layer, but it covers only part of the requested scope. On the product side: iMotions ships all four in-scope modalities (webcam eye tracking, facial expression, voice, respiration) from a consumer webcam and microphone alone, but only the gaze engine (WebET 3.0) and the respiration algorithm are in-house — facial coding is Affectiva AFFDEX and voice is audEERING devAIce, the latter built on openSMILE, which makes openSMILE/eGeMAPS the technically correct open-source substitute; critically, iMotions' respiration is chest-motion ROI tracking, NOT rPPG, contradicting the framing assumed in the research question. "Browser-first" is true only of the participant side: capture happens in the browser, then a study zip is imported into a Windows-only, GPU-backed desktop Lab for processing (Automated AOI requires an RTX card), and the lab/external-sensor integration surface is a socket event bus (TCP/UDP/LSL), not REST or WebSocket. On validation: peer-reviewed work converges on webcam gaze being roughly 2x worse than research-grade IR (WebGazer 24% vs Tobii 13% screen-size offset in smooth pursuit; Kaduk et al. ~1.4 deg vs EyeLink ~0.9 deg), with AOI hit rates of 70-76% vs 95% for IR, a ~200 ms cluster-onset delay, and 9-39 fps jittered sampling — yet substantive experimental effects still replicate with 20-27% attenuation, and parity holds only on large displays with simple, coarse-AOI stimuli. Major caveat: this verified claim set contains essentially no evidence on facial AU accuracy vs certified FACS coders, speech-emotion benchmarks, rPPG-vs-ECG/spirometry validation, demographic bias, or any of the study-design, ethics, GDPR/BIPA/EU AI Act and statistical-analysis material the brief requested — those sections of the deliverable are currently unsourced and need a second research pass.

### 4. [supported within 3-0 and 2-1 claims] (confidence: medium)

**Claim:** CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF'S PREMISE: iMotions' webcam respiration is chest-motion ROI tracking, explicitly NOT remote photoplethysmography. ### 6. [3-0 across four merged claims] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Webcam gaze carries a large, well-quantified accuracy penalty versus research-grade infrared, but aggregate experimental effects still replicate with attenuation — the penalty constrains AOI granularity and effect-size budgets rather than invalidating the method.

**Evidence:** Merged from four independently verified claims. (1) Slim, Kandel, Yacovone & Snedeker 2024, Open Mind (CC BY, PMC11627531), within-session smooth-pursuit task on the same 30 participants: grand mean Euclidean offset 13% of screen size (SD 10) for Tobii T60 infrared vs 24% (SD 14) for WebGazer, t(29) = -9.74, p < .001 — roughly double. (2) Same paper, fixation task AOI hit rates: target-quadrant look proportion peaked at 95% (infrared), 97% lab / 92% web (HUMAN manual annotation of the same webcam video), and only 76% lab / 70% web (WebGazer); WebGazer's significant cluster began at 300 ms vs 100 ms for infrared, a ~200 ms (2-bin) onset lag. Because manual annotation of the SAME video reached 92%, the deficit is attributable to the gaze-estimation ALGORITHM, not to webcam capture per se — a better estimator can recover much of the gap. Note this was the paper's easiest, largest-effect task, so 70-76% is a near-ceiling best case. (3) Wisiecka et al., ETRA '22 (n = 83): 'Despite higher measurement error of webcam eye tracking, our results in all three experimental conditions were in line with theoretical expectations' — the happiness-superiority effect in TTFF and the ambient-to-focal attention switch both replicated. (4) Degen et al. (Behavior Research Methods, PMC11289066) replicated the cascade effect, novelty preference and visual world paradigm online with WebGazer, with effect sizes shrunk 20-27%. (5) For a degrees-of-visual-angle figure cite Kaduk et al. 2023 (~1.4 deg accuracy / 1.1 deg precision webcam vs ~0.9 deg EyeLink), NOT the 24%/13% screen-proportion figures, which are a moving-target measure conflating spatial error with temporal lag. PLANNING RULE: budget ~20-30% effect attenuation and inflate sample size accordingly; size AOIs to tolerate ~1.5-3 deg error plus a ~25-30% miss rate.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11627531/ | https://direct.mit.edu/opmi/article/doi/10.1162/opmi_a_00171/125551/Webcams-as-Windows-to-the-Mind-A-Direct-Comparison | https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3517031.3529615 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11289066/


### 7. [3-0 and 2-1 (merged)] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Webcam gaze reaches parity with lab hardware only under favourable conditions — large displays and simple, low-density stimuli — and degrades systematically with smaller screens and denser choice matrices, making AOI size in degrees and stimulus density the primary design constraints for remote gaze studies.

**Evidence:** Munoz, Maksimenko, Henriquez-Jara, Bansal & Perez (2025), 'In-lab versus web-based eye-tracking in decision-making: A systematic comparison on multiple display-size conditions mimicking common electronic devices', Behavior Research Methods, DOI 10.3758/s13428-025-02867-0, PMID 41214374, published 2025-11-10 (paywalled; abstract retrieved via NCBI efetch). Two choice experiments varied display size and task difficulty (simple pizza choice vs complex multi-attribute travel-mode choice) against an EyeLink 1000 Plus: 'on larger displays and simpler tasks, WebGazer produces gaze patterns and parameter inferences from computational models of behavior comparable to EyeLink', with reliability deteriorating significantly on smaller screens and with more complex choice matrices. THREE SCOPE CORRECTIONS the build guide must carry: (a) the ordering monitor > laptop > tablet > mobile is extrapolation — the abstract supports only 'smaller displays'; (b) 'AOI size and stimulus density' is this report's mechanistic gloss, the authors frame it as display size and choice-matrix complexity; (c) findings are specific to WebGazer.js — RealEye, GazeRecorder, Tobii Sticky, EyeLogic and iMotions WebET were not tested and may differ. Corroborating boundary case: 'On current limitations of online eye-tracking to study the visual processing of source code' (Information and Software Technology, 2024) finds webcam precision insufficient for word-level reading AOIs. The paper reports comparability of gaze patterns and choice-model parameters, not raw spatial error, so do not cite it for a degrees figure.

**Sources:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-025-02867-0 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41214374/


### 8. [3-0 (with verifier-flagged overreach)] (confidence: medium)

**Claim:** Moving a study from lab to participants' homes added little or no additional noise for a WebGazer-class pipeline — suggesting the accuracy ceiling is the algorithm rather than the uncontrolled home environment — but this rests on a between-subjects comparison with a Mac-only remote sample and should be treated as encouraging, not settled.

**Evidence:** Slim et al. 2024: WebGazer mean Euclidean offset was 24% of screen size both in-lab (n=30) and over the web (n=42), t(67) = 0.43, p = .680. The authors' own wording is weaker than the claim as originally framed: 'web-based testing appeared to add little to no additional noise'. FOUR REASONS TO HEDGE: (1) the lab-vs-web contrast is BETWEEN-subjects — different people, different hardware — so a non-significant difference in an unmatched comparison is not evidence of environmental invariance, and 'the ceiling is the algorithm' is the claim author's causal inference, not a tested result; (2) remote participants were REQUIRED to use a Mac (MacBook Pro n=20, MacBook Air n=20, iMac n=2) on Chrome/Firefox because PCIbex's WebGazer would not run in Safari, so the 'home environment' tested is not the arbitrary consumer Windows/webcam population a real deployment faces; (3) the two conditions differed on sampling rate (12 vs 26 fps) and screen size (13.3-27 in), making the matching means partly coincidental; (4) the web experiment had real data loss — manual-annotation data unanalysable for 22 participants due to processing issues, 2 more dropped for stimulus-presentation problems — which is a direct input to remote dropout budgeting.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11627531/ | https://direct.mit.edu/opmi/article/doi/10.1162/opmi_a_00171/125551/Webcams-as-Windows-to-the-Mind-A-Direct-Comparison


### 9. [3-0] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Browser gaze pipelines must treat sampling rate as a measured per-session variable, not a constant: WebGazer achieved 12 fps on one lab machine (SD 0.4, range 11-13) and 26 fps across home setups (SD 8.9, range 9-39) versus 60 fps for the infrared reference — so assume ~10-40 Hz jittered sampling, bin to ~100 ms, and validate the achieved rate at session start.

**Evidence:** All five figures verified verbatim against the PMC full text. The 100 ms binning prescription is the source's own analysis choice ('We analyzed gaze locations in bins of 100 ms'), used as the standard temporal unit for cluster-based permutation analyses — not an extrapolation by the reporter. jsPsych's official WebGazer extension independently corroborates both the mechanism and the mitigation: the achievable sampling rate 'will depend on the computing power of the participant's device', and its validation routine returns a samples_per_sec metric you are advised to check. ENGINEERING CONSEQUENCES: (a) never assume fixed dt — timestamp every sample and resample or bin; (b) velocity-based saccade detection and any microsaccade or saccade-velocity metric is off the table at these rates, which is why dispersion- or HMM-style fixation classification is the correct choice; (c) log per-session effective fps as a data-quality screening variable with a pre-registered exclusion threshold; (d) these numbers are WebGazer-specific and 2024-vintage — a modern MediaPipe/WASM or WebGPU pipeline is not bound by them and could exceed 40 Hz on capable hardware, so measure rather than inherit the ceiling. The defensible general core is: rate is device- and load-dependent, varies several-fold across participants, and must be measured per session.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11627531/ | https://direct.mit.edu/opmi/article/doi/10.1162/opmi_a_00171/125551/Webcams-as-Windows-to-the-Mind-A-Direct-Comparison | https://www.jspsych.org/latest/extensions/webgazer/


### 10. [2-1] (confidence: medium)

**Claim:** Calibration point density is a first-order design parameter with clear diminishing returns: raising joint calibration/evaluation density from 5 to 18 points cut mean spatial error ~27% (1383 to 1009 device px) and mean angular error ~24% (13.556 to 10.322 deg), with the largest single step from 9 to 12 points and only marginal gain from 12 to 18 — so use at least 12 calibration points and spend the remaining effort elsewhere.

**Evidence:** 'Systematic Performance Evaluation of WebGazer Webcam-Based Eye Tracking with Spatial and Angular Error Analysis', Journal of Eye Movement Research 19(5):99, 8 Sep 2026 (MDPI blocks direct fetch; retrieved via text-extraction proxy and cross-checked against the published abstract). Numbers verified verbatim, and the diminishing-returns result is explicit: 9-9 to 12-12 reduces mean spatial error by 186 px (15.2%) and angular error by 1.737 deg (14.2%), whereas 12-12 to 18-18 yields only 28 px and 0.213 deg. FOUR REASONS THIS IS MEDIUM: (1) calibration and evaluation point counts were varied JOINTLY (5-5, 9-9, 12-12, 18-18), so the effect cannot be attributed to calibration density alone — evaluation-grid geometry changed too, and reading it as 'more calibration points -> better gaze' over-reads the design; (2) the absolute errors (10.3-13.6 deg mean angular) are extreme outliers versus the ~4 deg commonly reported for WebGazer (Papoutsaki et al. 2016) and 1-2 deg for commercial trackers, so the relative-improvement curve may not transfer to a better-tuned pipeline; (3) n = 20 over 380 runs, single site, single device (Dell XPS 13 Plus 9320, integrated webcam, 1280x720 @ 30 fps), with the authors noting head movement, camera positioning and lighting were not physically constrained; (4) MDPI venue, single unreplicated study. Also note the supporting quote originally attached to this claim (about which factor showed the widest error range) is a different sentence and does not support the numbers — cite the 27.0% and 12-12-vs-18-18 sentences instead. A related claim that target presentation duration is the dominant driver of angular error was voted down 0-3 and should not be used.

**Sources:** https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/19/5/99

### 12. [3-0] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** For the secondary in-lab/desktop mode, the incumbent's integration surface is a socket-based text-event bus — TCP (iMotions listens as server), UDP (one datagram per event), or LSL as a licensed add-on — not REST or WebSocket; browsers cannot reach it directly, and third-party projects exist solely to bridge WebSocket to it.

## Open questions

1. What is the independently measured accuracy, in degrees of visual angle, of modern non-WebGazer browser gaze estimators (MediaPipe Face Landmarker plus a learned gaze head, WebGPU inference, or commercial WebET/RealEye) under uncontrolled home conditions — and does the ~92% human-annotation ceiling on identical webcam video found by Slim et al. mean a better algorithm can close most of the gap to infrared without better hardware?
2. What is the peer-reviewed accuracy of automated AU detection (AFFDEX, OpenFace 2.x, Py-Feat) against certified FACS coders, of speech-emotion models against human-rated benchmarks (IEMOCAP, MSP-Podcast), and of rPPG-derived HR/HRV against ECG in remote settings — and how large are the documented skin-tone, age and gender performance disparities across all four modalities? None of this survived into the current evidence base.
3. What is the actual legal exposure for browser-based emotion recognition: how do GDPR Art. 9 special-category biometric rules, Illinois BIPA consent and retention requirements, and the EU AI Act's prohibition on emotion inference in workplace and education contexts constrain the addressable use cases, and what consent, retention, on-device-processing and data-residency architecture is required to stay compliant?
4. How should asynchronous streams at radically different and jittering rates (gaze 9-39 Hz, facial AUs at frame rate, audio 16-48 kHz, respiration ~1 Hz effective) be time-aligned, epoched to stimulus events, and quality-screened in a browser with no hardware clock — specifically what clock-offset correction, per-session fps logging, and pre-registered exclusion thresholds should the platform enforce automatically?
5. What is inside the iMotions Online-to-Lab study zip, and more usefully, what open interchange format should a rebuild adopt instead — a BIDS-like directory with per-signal Parquet/CSV and a canonical event table, or LSL XDF — so that collected data survives independently of the platform that produced it?

## Caveats

SOURCE-TYPE ASYMMETRY: every iMotions architecture and feature finding rests on vendor-authored pages or brochures. That is the correct and only primary source for feature-existence and supplier-attribution claims (a vendor does not falsely credit a competitor's engine), but it is worthless for accuracy. No peer-reviewed validation of iMotions' own WebET 3.0 or Webcam Respiration was retrieved: the WebET validation study is gated behind a form, the "~50% of participants at <=2 dva" figure circulating in vendor material is unaudited, and the respiration ICC 0.815 / 0.739 figures come from an iMotions-authored ResearchGate white paper. Flag all of these explicitly as unverified vendor claims in the deliverable, and never let the RDC feature list carry an accuracy implication.

ALGORITHM-SPECIFIC GENERALIZATION: five of the seven gaze-validation findings test WebGazer.js specifically, the low-fidelity open-source end of the field. Commercial webcam trackers (iMotions WebET, RealEye, GazeRecorder, Tobii Sticky, EyeLogic) and modern MediaPipe/WASM/WebGPU pipelines were not tested and may perform materially better — attribute the numbers to WebGazer and treat them as a conservative floor for a 2026 rebuild rather than a ceiling. The strongest internal evidence for this is that human annotation of the same webcam video hit 92% target detection where WebGazer hit 70%.

MEASURE MISMATCH: the headline 24%/13% figures are Euclidean offset as a proportion of screen size during a MOVING-target task, conflating spatial error with temporal lag; do not quote them as degrees of visual angle. Use Kaduk et al. 2023 (~1.4 deg vs ~0.9 deg) for dva. The MDPI calibration study's absolute errors (10-14 dva) are roughly an order of magnitude worse than the WebGazer literature baseline and should be cited only for the relative shape of the density curve, never as an accuracy figure.

DESIGN LIMITS OF THE KEY STUDIES: the lab-vs-web null is between-subjects with a Mac-only remote sample and unequal sampling rates; the ETRA '22 study is between-subjects, lab-administered (not at-home), and uses coarse AOIs; the Munoz et al. findings sit inside a discrete-choice paradigm and may not transfer to free-viewing ads or websites; the calibration study confounds calibration and evaluation point counts and used n=20 on a single laptop.

ACCESS AND PROCESS FAILURES: ACM DL, Springer, MIT Press and MDPI all returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so several claims were verified via PMC, Semantic Scholar or NCBI efetch mirrors rather than publisher text, and some body-text details (including any dva figures in Wisiecka et al.) remain unread. Two verifiers exhausted their web-search budget before running adversarial contradiction searches, so the "no contradicting evidence" leg is untested for the display-size and smooth-pursuit findings. Several claims also arrived with supporting quotes that did not match the numbers they were attached to (notably the calibration-density claim) — the numbers checked out against the source but the citation hygiene did not, so quotes must be re-pulled before publication.

NORMATIVE CLAUSES ARE INFERENCE, NOT SOURCES: the "so a rebuild must..." tail on several claims (route external sensors through the same path, expose this control surface, treat modalities as arousal proxies) is report-author design reasoning. Present it as synthesis, not as sourced vendor or academic fact.

TIME SENSITIVITY AND CORPORATE STRUCTURE: iMotions product facts were checked live on 2026-09-20 against Lab 11.1.11 and an April-2026-valid brochure, so they are current but will drift. The webcam-eye-tracking accuracy literature is improving year over year, so 2024-2025 figures should be re-baselined before being used to set acceptance criteria. Finally, Smart Eye owns iMotions, Affectiva and therefore AFFDEX — "third-party engine" does not mean "available to you on the same terms".
