## Summary

On the two topics where pass 2 produced verified evidence, the answer is consistently unfavorable to the iMotions-equivalent feature set, and the failure is architectural rather than a matter of picking a better engine. For facial analysis, the AU-detection layer is merely modest (OpenFace mean AUC 0.72 in-the-wild, mean per-AU F1 .52-.59 on DISFA Plus, with AU14/AU23 at 0.56-0.58 AUC and AU20 at F1 .13-.24), while the AU-to-emotion layer that AFFDEX-style products sell is rejected outright by the field's consensus review — Barrett et al. (2019) find coherence of only r = .32 with the hypothesized configuration present in ~19% of emotional episodes, and Py-Feat's own authors endorse that caveat; eight commercial classifiers averaged 53.9% emotion accuracy against 72.5% for human raters on the same 937 videos, with Affectiva's Affdex at 50.5%. Engine choice alone swings usable data by ~20 percentage points (iMotions 43% valid face frames vs FaceReader 63%, Py-Feat 62%) and changes absolute expression counts by 3-4x on identical video, so no single engine's absolute output is ground truth — though relative, within-participant proportions did prove robust across engines, which is the one constructive design signal in this evidence. For voice, naturalistic 8-class SER tops out at ~0.36 Macro-F1 (chance 0.125), cross-corpus valence CCC falls 30%, and the headline transformer valence result is driven by implicit lexical content learned in fine-tuning — meaning it evaporates under the codec compression, noise suppression and raw-audio-discarding privacy architecture such a platform would need. Critically, pass 2 evidenced nothing on rPPG/respiration, bias quantification, the metrics and synchronization pipeline, study-design thresholds, or legal compliance; those five topics remain as unaddressed as they were after pass 1.

## Findings

### 1. [3-0 on all four constituent claims (0, 1, 2, 7)] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** The AU-to-basic-emotion mapping layer that AFFDEX-style pipelines depend on is scientifically invalid as a diagnostic inference, and the leading open-source toolbox authors concede the point in their own paper.

**Evidence:** Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez & Pollak (2019), Psychological Science in the Public Interest 20(1):1-68 (invited consensus review, deliberately spanning opposing camps) concludes verbatim that "instances of these emotion categories are NOT expressed with facial movements that are sufficiently reliable and specific across contexts, individuals, and cultures to be considered diagnostic displays of any emotional state", and that "a given configuration of facial movements, such as a scowl, often communicates something other than an emotional state". The paper names Affectiva and Microsoft's Emotion API explicitly. Quantitatively, it summarizes Duran & Fernandez-Dols's meta-analysis (89 effect sizes, 47 studies, 3,599 participants, spontaneous facial behavior): average coherence r = .32 (per-category range .25-.38, 'weak evidence of reliability') and the hypothesized configuration present in only ~19% of emotional episodes (per-category range .15-.25, 'no evidence to weak evidence'). Note: an earlier harness quote mis-spliced these two ranges; cite the clauses separately. Barrett et al. credit automated AU *detection* as accurate (>90% vs expert coders) under ideal lab conditions while locating the failure in the AU->emotion inference layer — i.e. exactly the AFFDEX architecture's second stage. The Py-Feat authors (Cheong et al., Affective Science 2023) cite Barrett et al. affirmatively for the caveat that "detecting a smiling face as happy does not necessarily imply that the individual is experiencing an internal subjective state of happiness ... as these types of latent state inferences require additional contextual information beyond a static image", and state "there is currently no consensus in the field if categorical representations of emotion are the most reliable and valid nosology". IMPORTANT SCOPING: Barrett et al. concede above-chance association ('more than what would be expected by chance'); the defensible statement is 'not reliable or specific enough to be diagnostic', NOT 'carries no information'. Py-Feat likewise still ships categorical emotion detectors and argues the labels remain descriptively useful.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856/ | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10751270/


### 2. [3-0 on claims 3, 5, 6] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Automated AU detection against FACS-coded ground truth is modest (mean AUC ~0.72, mean per-AU F1 ~0.52-0.59) and collapses for specific subtle AUs, so a webcam platform can trust only a handful of high-amplitude AUs.

**Evidence:** Namba, Sato, Osumi & Shimokawa (2021, Sensors), cross-domain benchmark against frame-by-frame FACS coding, OpenFace 2.0 mean AUC: 0.72 (SD 0.11) on Aff-Wild2 (in-the-wild), 0.81 (SD 0.10) on DISFA+ (posed), 0.72 (SD 0.11) on GFT (spontaneous conversation). Same paper: "detection of certain facial movements such as AU14 (dimpler: average AUC = 0.58) and 23 (lip tightener: average AUC = 0.56) seems difficult for automatic AU detection systems" (averages across 3 systems x 3 databases); the authors attribute this to those AUs reflecting facial *tension* rather than movement, needing depth/3D/blood-flow cues. By contrast AU12 exceeds 0.80 and AU6 exceeds 0.90 on posed data. Py-Feat paper Table 6 (DISFA Plus, F1): average .59 FACET/iMotions, .57 OpenFace, .52 Feat-XGB, .52 Feat-SVM; per-AU spread is enormous — AU12 .72-.83, AU25 .64-.83, versus AU20 .13-.24, AU17 .25-.47, AU15 .22-.59. Py-Feat's default model (Feat-XGB) was chosen because it "provides AU detection probability estimates rather than binary classifications", not for accuracy. CORRECTIONS to carry forward: AUC 0.72 is conventionally 'fair/acceptable', not 'chance-to-moderate' — the real story is the per-AU floor, not the mean. Feat-XGB and Feat-SVM tie at .52, so XGB is not 'lower-F1' than the alternative. Neither paper reports a human inter-coder baseline in these tables, so 'far from certified-coder agreement' is inference. Independent corroboration for AU23 difficulty: ME-GraphAU (arXiv:2205.01782) BP4D within-corpus AU23 F1 ~50, still worst-in-class for 2022 SOTA trained in-domain. Currency caveat: OpenFace 2.0 / 2021-era tools; OpenFace 3.0 (2025) and newer transformer AU models are untested here.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8235167/ | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10751270/


### 3. [3-0 on claims 9, 10, 11; 2-1 on claim 4 (FaceReader)] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Commercial and open-source facial-affect classifiers substantially underperform human observers on emotion recognition (53.9% vs 72.5%), and the gap widens from posed to spontaneous expression — so lab benchmarks overstate what a webcam platform will achieve.

**Evidence:** Dupre, Krumhuber, Kuster & McKeown (2020, PLOS ONE 15(4):e0231968) tested eight commercial classifiers (Affectiva Affdex, CrowdEmotion, Emotient FACET, Microsoft Cognitive Services, MorphCast, Neurodata Lab, VicarVision FaceReader, Visage) on the same 937 videos (467 posed BU-4DFE, 470 spontaneous UT-Dallas) rated by 14 human observers: "human observers generally performed better than the automatic classifiers (human observers: M = 72.48, 95%CI = [71.72;73.24] vs. automatic classifiers: M = 53.88, 95%CI = [52.75;55.01])". Per-classifier means: Emotient FACET 61.90, VicarVision FaceReader 57.31, Neurodata 56.78, Visage 55.07, Microsoft 52.61, Affectiva Affdex 50.48 (6th of 8), MorphCast 48.56, CrowdEmotion 48.35. Posed-vs-spontaneous: "performance by the two best performing classifiers approximated those of human observers, suggesting high agreement for posed expressions. However, classification accuracy was consistently lower (although above chance level) for spontaneous affective behavior." Separately, Namba et al. found commercial FaceReader 7 AUC 0.55 (SD 0.05) on DISFA+ and 0.58 (SD 0.12) on GFT — barely above chance — versus 0.81/0.72 for OpenFace, though on Aff-Wild2 FaceReader (0.67) beat AFAR (0.65) and nearly matched OpenFace (0.72), so the commercial-underperformance gap is dataset-specific. Corroborated by Skiendziel, Rosch & Schultheiss (2019, PLoS ONE) finding FaceReader 7 AU-level weighted F1 = 0.63 vs certified FACS coding. HARD CAVEATS: (a) versions are 2018-2019 vintage — Affdex was SDK v3.4.1, not the AFFDEX 2.0 that iMotions ships post-Smart-Eye acquisition; the paper itself warns "Results may be subject to change with the release of newer versions"; Emotient FACET is discontinued (Apple, 2016), FaceReader is now v9/v10 and the Namba authors said v7 should be updated to 8.1 before comparison. (b) Both corpora are lab-recorded, well-lit and frontal — NOT webcam — so these are an upper bound on webcam performance, not a webcam measurement. (c) This is 6-way basic-emotion classification accuracy, not per-AU F1. (d) AFFDEX was not tested by Namba et al.; do not transfer the FaceReader number to AFFDEX. (e) UT-Dallas classes are severely unbalanced (anger n=3 vs happiness n=196).

**Sources:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0231968 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8235167/


### 4. [3-0 on claims 12, 13, 14, 15] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** On the same naturalistic video, the three leading engines disagree so much on absolute output that no single engine's expression counts or face-detection rate can be treated as ground truth — though relative/proportional group-level conclusions were robust.

**Evidence:** Manelis-Baram et al. (2025), Molecular Autism 16:50, doi:10.1186/s13229-025-00685-x: >5 million frames from 100 children aged 2-7 (72 autistic, 28 TD) during ~45-min ADOS-2 sessions, all three engines run on the SAME recordings. (a) Sensitivity: "Py-Feat identified emotions in three to four times as many frames as FaceReader and iMotions" (per-emotion it is not a flat factor — happiness: Py-Feat M=18.74, FaceReader M=11.35, iMotions M=4.41). (b) Face detection: iMotions/AFFDEX valid face frames M = 0.43 (SD 0.16) vs FaceReader M = 0.63 (SD 0.16) and Py-Feat M = 0.62 (SD 0.17), F(2,294) = 55.68, p < 0.001 — a ~20-point difference in usable data purely from engine choice, with no explanation offered and no software versions or confidence thresholds reported. (c) Per-child agreement on happiness proportion: FaceReader-iMotions r = 0.75 (autism) / 0.64 (control), FaceReader-Py-Feat r = 0.37 / 0.44, iMotions-Py-Feat r = 0.37 / 0.55; authors call this "relatively weak agreement across algorithms". (d) Authors' own framing: "Significant variability in the output of the three facial analysis algorithms highlights the current limitations of automated facial expression recognition", with an explicit call for "large, annotated video repositories that can serve as ground truth datasets". CRITICAL QUALIFICATIONS: the study has NO FACS ground truth — "we did not compare the results from each of the algorithms to manually annotated data" — so this is inter-algorithm reliability, not validity; 'sensitivity' means detection RATE, not true-positive rate, and it cannot be cited as evidence Py-Feat is more accurate. The paper's other half, in its own title ('but consistent proportions'), is that all three engines agreed there was no autistic/TD difference in expression quantity — relative measures were robust where absolute ones were not. Population is preschool children in clinical interaction with a wall-mounted camera, a much harder case than a seated adult at a laptop; the 43% figure must not be quoted as an expected webcam face-detection rate. Control-group r values rest on n=28.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12512823/


### 5. [3-0 on claim 8, but single source and qualitative] (confidence: medium)

**Claim:** Demographic bias is conceded by toolbox authors at the face-detection stage — darker-pigmented faces are harder to detect — but the affective-computing literature surfaced here provides no quantified skin-tone-stratified benchmark.

**Evidence:** Py-Feat Limitations section, verbatim: "Third, our models likely contain some degree of bias with respect to gender and race. ... This problem is inherent to the field of affective computing and will only improve as datasets increase in diversity and representation and preprocessing pipelines improve (e.g., faces with darker pigmentation are often more difficult to detect; Nagpal et al., 2019; Rhue, 2018)." This is an admission against interest from the toolbox's own authors in a peer-reviewed venue, which is the strongest source class for a concession. Architecturally it matters because Py-Feat's pipeline is strictly sequential (face detection -> landmarks -> AU -> emotion), so a missed face yields no downstream output at all. LIMITS OF THE EVIDENCE: the concession is qualitative and cites external work (Nagpal 2019 on face-recognition bias; Rhue 2018 on racial influence on automated emotion perception) rather than reporting Py-Feat's own per-Fitzpatrick detection or AU-F1 numbers — no stratified figures exist in this source. The extension to camera-based physiological measures (rPPG) is pure inference: Py-Feat contains no rPPG and the paper says nothing about it. This finding must not be presented as covering the brief's section 4; the rPPG/Fitzpatrick, eye-tracking-with-glasses/epicanthic-fold, and SER-accent-bias literatures are entirely unevidenced by this pass.

**Sources:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10751270/


### 6. [3-0 on claim 16] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** The modern transformer valence result in speech emotion recognition is driven by implicit lexical content learned during fine-tuning, not by paralinguistics — so any pipeline that degrades intelligible speech (heavy codecs, aggressive noise suppression, de-identification, or hand-crafted eGeMAPS/ComParE features) will not reproduce it.

**Evidence:** Wagner, Triantafyllopoulos, Wierstorf, Schmitt, Burkhardt, Eyben, Schuller & Dawn (arXiv:2203.07378, published IEEE TPAMI 2023), abstract verbatim: "we are the first to show that their extraordinary success on valence is based on implicit linguistic information learnt during fine-tuning of the transformer layers, which explains why they perform on-par with recent multimodal approaches that explicitly utilise textual information". Mechanism demonstrated directly, not inferred: Sec. 4.3 re-synthesised MSP-Podcast test utterances via TTS from transcripts (same words, prosody stripped) and high-valence models retained high correlation on the flat synthetic audio (w2v2-L-robust CCC .635 on originals); when transformer layers are FROZEN during fine-tuning, correlation with synthesised speech drops to almost zero. Sec. 4.2: fusing explicit BERT embeddings from ASR transcripts gave no improvement for the best models. Corroborated by the companion probing study arXiv:2204.00400, where valence predictions track sentiment words and negations in neutral-prosody synthesised speech while arousal and dominance are unaffected (same group, so partial rather than independent replication). Degradation table Sec. 4.8: valence CCC falls -0.221 under natural-soundscape noise and -0.278 under human non-speech noise at 0 dB SNR, versus ~0 for fades and -0.062 for speed change. WORDING CORRECTION: say 'not paralinguistic', NOT 'not acoustic' — the model's only input is the waveform, and the paper's headline is that it reaches CCC .638 valence on MSP-Podcast "without use of explicit linguistic information". A reader could otherwise wrongly conclude an ASR/text branch is needed, which the paper explicitly contradicts. Design implication: audio-only end-to-end SER is viable only if speech stays intelligible and is retained — which collides directly with the privacy architecture (discard raw audio, keep only derived features) that section 7 of the brief contemplates.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07378


### 7. [3-0 on claim 18; 2-1 on claim 17 (cross-corpus), verifier confidence high on both] (confidence: high)

**Claim:** Speech emotion recognition numbers do not transfer across corpora, and absolute categorical performance on naturalistic speech is very weak — best-in-challenge Macro-F1 ~0.36 on 8 classes against 0.125 chance.

**Evidence:** CROSS-CORPUS: Wagner et al. fine-tuned on MSP-Podcast only and tested on IEMOCAP and MOSI. w2v2-L-robust reached CCC .745/.634 (arousal/dominance) and .635 valence on MSP-Podcast, falling to .663/.518 arousal/dominance and .448 valence on IEMOCAP, and .539 for sentiment on MOSI. The authors quantify it themselves: "The drop in CCC between in-domain and cross-corpus results for w2v2-L-robust on IEMOCAP is 11% for arousal, 21% for dominance, and 30% for valence ... and 15% for sentiment on MOSI" — i.e. degradation is dimension-dependent, with valence worst, and the authors' own rhetorical framing is that transformers generalize BETTER than CNN baselines. MSP-Podcast (natural podcast audio) -> IEMOCAP (acted dyadic lab recordings) is exactly a population/environment shift of the kind a new webcam study population represents. ABSOLUTE PERFORMANCE: Goncalves et al., Odyssey 2024 Emotion Recognition Challenge (ISCA Archive), MSP-Podcast spontaneous speech, 8 emotion classes, class-BALANCED test set (chance = 0.125 by arithmetic, not stated in paper), 31 teams submitted to Task 1. WavLM-Large baseline (microsoft/wavlm-large, 24 layers, 310M params) scored Macro-F1 0.3113 / Micro-F1 0.3272 per Tables 2 and 4, ranking 13th. Top-3: "Sheffield-MINI with 0.3569 (.046 over Baseline), TalTech with 0.3543 (.043 over Baseline), and UPC-BSC with 0.3441 (.033 over Baseline)." DOCUMENTED PAPER ERROR: the Sec. 4.2 prose says "a F1-micro score of 0.311 and a F1-macro score of 0.327", which swaps the labels relative to both tables — cite the tables. Scope this to the Odyssey 2024 challenge under its constraints rather than to SER in general; later MSP-Podcast work may report higher Macro-F1.

## Open questions

1. What is the independently measured accuracy of rPPG heart rate (MAE in bpm) versus ECG on UBFC-rPPG, PURE, VIPL-HR and MMPD, are HRV metrics (RMSSD, SDNN, LF/HF) recoverable at consumer webcam frame rates, and how severely do browser video compression and frame-rate jitter degrade the signal? This was a named pass-2 priority and produced zero surviving claims — it is the single largest evidentiary hole and directly determines whether a respiration/physiology module is buildable at all.
2. What are the quantified demographic disparities across the four modalities — Fitzpatrick skin-type effects on rPPG and face detection, race/gender/age disparities in expression recognition (including the reported finding that Black faces are assigned more negative emotion), eye-tracking performance with glasses and varying eyelid morphology, and SER bias by accent and L2 speech? Only a single qualitative vendor-side concession survived, with no stratified numbers and no named audit datasets.
3. What is the current legal exposure envelope for a browser-based emotion-inference product — specifically the EU AI Act Article 5(1)(f) prohibition scope and compliance dates, Annex III high-risk classification triggers, GDPR Article 9 lawful bases and DPIA obligations, and BIPA's written-consent/retention requirements with documented damages? Nothing was evidenced, and the Barrett et al. finding is precisely the scientific basis regulators cite, so the technical and legal findings are coupled.
4. Given that the verified valence signal in transformer SER is lexical rather than paralinguistic, and that Barrett et al. invalidate AU-to-emotion mapping as diagnostic, what derived measures should the platform actually ship? The evidence supports AU-level and prosody-level descriptive outputs with per-AU reliability disclosed, plus relative/within-participant comparisons (which the Molecular Autism study found robust even where absolute rates diverged 3-4x) — but no source here establishes which specific composite indices would survive scrutiny, nor what a defensible valence/arousal/engagement definition looks like when acoustics alone cannot deliver valence and a single smile-related AU cannot carry it either.
5. Do post-2023 AU detectors (OpenFace 3.0, transformer/graph AU models, MediaPipe blendshapes, AFFDEX 2.0) materially change the per-AU reliability picture, and is there any webcam-recorded, FACS-ground-truthed validation dataset at all? Every benchmark in this set predates those releases and none used webcam video.

## Caveats

COVERAGE IS THE DOMINANT CAVEAT. Pass 2 was asked to evidence seven topics; the 19 surviving claims cover topic 1 (facial/AU validation) thoroughly, topic 2 (voice/SER) partially, and topics 3, 5, 6 and 7 not at all, with topic 4 (bias) represented only by a single qualitative concession. Specifically UNEVIDENCED and still open: all rPPG and respiration validation (UBFC-rPPG, PURE, VIPL-HR, MMPD, CHROM/POS/DeepPhys/PhysNet, HRV recoverability, compression/frame-rate-jitter effects); eGeMAPS/ComParE feature-set composition and the effects of consumer microphones, getUserMedia AGC/noise-suppression/echo-cancellation, codecs and room acoustics; all of the analysis/metrics pipeline (gaze metric definitions, heatmap kernels, scanpath comparison, dynamic AOIs, LSL/browser clock synchronization, statistics, BIDS/XDF/Parquet export); all of study design and participant running (calibration protocols, exclusion thresholds, attrition, power, panel quality); and all legal/ethical compliance (GDPR Art. 9/4(14), BIPA, CUBI, MHMDA, EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) and Annex III, IRB, retention architecture). Do not let the density of section-1 evidence disguise these gaps — several, particularly the EU AI Act emotion-inference prohibition and BIPA's private right of action, are gating for a commercial build.

VERIFICATION METHOD LIMITATION. Every verifier reported the session WebSearch budget exhausted (200/200), so no claim in this set received a completed adversarial contradiction sweep. All 'not refuted' verdicts rest on direct re-fetching of the primary source rather than on a search for rebuttals. This is a strong test for claims that are transcriptions of what a named paper says (most of these are) and a weak test for claims asserting a general state of the field. Two attempted counter-source fetches (Cowen & Keltner, Nature 2021) were blocked by auth redirects, so the counter-literature to Barrett et al. was assessed from background knowledge only.

VERSION AND TIME SENSITIVITY. The commercial-classifier numbers are 2018-2020 SDK vintage: Affdex v3.4.1 (iMotions now ships AFFDEX 2.0 post-Smart-Eye acquisition), FaceReader v7 (now v9/v10), Emotient FACET discontinued. Namba et al. evaluated OpenFace 2.0 in 2021; OpenFace 3.0 shipped in 2025 and modern graph/transformer AU models are untested in any source here. Py-Feat has shipped newer detectors since the 2023 paper. None of these figures should be quoted in the present tense as current vendor performance; all should be dated and versioned.

POPULATION AND MODALITY MISMATCH. No source in this set measured webcam data. BU-4DFE and UT-Dallas are lab-recorded, well-lit and frontal; Aff-Wild2 is curated YouTube; DISFA+ is posed; GFT is lab conversation; the Molecular Autism study used a wall-mounted camera on 2-7-year-olds in clinical assessment. These bracket the webcam case rather than measure it — controlled-corpus figures are an optimistic upper bound and the child/ADOS figures a pessimistic lower bound. The honest statement is that webcam-specific AU and emotion validation numbers were not found.

THREE SPECIFIC WORDING TRAPS inherited from the claim set, corrected in the findings above but worth restating: (1) Barrett et al. concede above-chance signal, so 'invalid' means 'not diagnostic', never 'no information'. (2) OpenFace's 0.72 mean AUC is 'fair', not 'chance-level' — the per-AU floor at 0.56-0.58 is the real finding. (3) The Wagner et al. valence result is 'not paralinguistic', not 'not acoustic'; the model takes audio only and text fusion added nothing.

SIX CLAIMS WERE REFUTED and must not resurface: the 'only AU12 is trustworthy' generalization; the claimed occlusion/luminance/head-rotation degradation figures attributed to the Py-Feat paper (0-3 refuted); both FaceReader-9 AU-to-valence/arousal regression claims from PMC11341571; and both framings of the transformer valence result that asserted it as a clean acoustic SOTA advance (0-3 refuted) — the surviving, verified framing is claim 16's, that the gain is lexical.
